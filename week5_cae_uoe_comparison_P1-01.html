Use of English · Part 1 · Comparison Structures

Comparison Structures

Multiple choice cloze — degree adverbs, fixed collocations & complementation  ·  Two texts × 8 items

Exam Strategy
Trap 1 — Degree Adverb Discrimination
Several adverbs intensify comparatives — but they differ in degree, register, and collocational range. The wrong option is grammatically possible but doesn't fit the register or meaning of the surrounding text.
The results were _____ more significant than expected.
considerably ✔ — neutral, academic
hugely — too informal for this register
much — possible but weaker degree signal
Trap 2 — Fixed Collocation
Comparison expressions are often fixed — only one preposition or particle collocates correctly. Near-synonyms look plausible but break the fixed phrase.
The new model bears little resemblance _____ its predecessor.
to ✔ — bear resemblance to is fixed
with  from  of — none collocate
Trap 3 — Complementation Frame
Near-synonym comparison verbs require different prepositions or clause structures. The wrong option means roughly the same thing but demands a different grammatical frame than the sentence provides.
The two approaches differ _____ one another in important ways.
from ✔ — differ from is the fixed frame
with — differ with means to disagree
to — used with similar/identical, not differ
Trap 4 — Register & Connotation
All four options are grammatically possible, but one belongs to the wrong register or carries a connotation that contradicts the meaning or tone of the sentence.
This stands in _____ contrast to the optimism of the previous decade.
stark ✔ — precise C1 collocation for strong contrast
big — too informal
hard — wrong collocation entirely
⚡ Procedure: identify what follows the gap before reading the options — a preposition, a noun, a comparative adjective? That immediately filters out two options in most cases. Then use register and degree to choose between the remaining two.
Multiple Choice Cloze — Text 1 of 2
Read the full sentence before looking at the options. For each gap, decide which word fits the meaning, collocation, and grammar of the surrounding text. Only one answer is correct.
Urban Growth and Quality of Life Questions 1 – 8
A decade-long study of urban development across twelve European cities has found that the relationship between population growth and quality of life is 1_______ more complex than early models had suggested. The cities that performed best on wellbeing indicators bore 2_______ resemblance to the sprawling, car-dependent urban forms that dominated growth in the previous century. Researchers were careful to 3_______ between cities that had grown organically and those that had expanded through deliberate policy intervention, as the two trajectories produced 4_______ different outcomes for residents. The findings were notably 5_______ from what urban planners had previously assumed, challenging the long-held view that density alone determines liveability. By 6_______ , cities that had invested heavily in green infrastructure showed 7_______ the same improvements in resident satisfaction regardless of overall size, suggesting that the quality of public space may be 8_______ more important than population density in determining urban wellbeing.
1   …is _____ more complex than…
Correct: C — considerably
Trap: Degree adverb discrimination. All four modify adjectives or comparatives, but only considerably combines neutral academic register with a strong degree signal appropriate here. Vastly overstates the degree and is informal. Greatly collocates with verbs (greatly improved) not comparatives. Highly modifies adjectives directly (highly complex) but not comparative forms (highly more complex is ungrammatical).
2   …bore _____ resemblance to…
Correct: D — little
Trap: Fixed collocation. Bear little resemblance to and bear no resemblance to are the fixed expressions at C1. Resemblance is an uncountable noun here — scarce, slight, and minor collocate with countable or physical nouns but not with abstract uncountable nouns in this fixed phrase.
3   …careful to _____ between cities that…
Correct: B — distinguish
Trap: Complementation frame. The frame is verb + between. Distinguish between is the standard academic expression for making intellectual distinctions. Differentiate between also works — see note below. Separate takes from, not between. Contrast takes with or appears without a preposition when the object follows directly — contrast between is not the standard active frame.
4   …produced _____ different outcomes…
Correct: A — markedly
Trap: Degree adverb collocation. Markedly different is a fixed C1 collocation meaning noticeably or significantly different — standard in academic writing. Deeply different collocates with emotional or values-based differences, not research outcomes. Strongly different is not a standard collocation in formal English. Severely collocates with negative states (severely damaged) not with different.
5   …notably _____ from what planners had assumed…
Correct: D — different
Trap: Complementation frame. The frame is adjective + from. Different from is the standard frame in formal British English. Distinct from also takes from and means clearly separate — but distinct implies two things are categorically separate rather than merely different in degree, which is too strong here. Unlike is a preposition, not an adjective — it cannot follow notably. Separate from describes physical or categorical division, not the meaning required.
6   _____ , cities that had invested in green…
Correct: A — contrast
Trap: Fixed collocation. By contrast is the fixed discourse marker introducing a contrasting point — it is always by, not in. Note: in contrast exists but takes to immediately after (in contrast to X). Here the gap is a standalone discourse marker with a comma following it, so by contrast is the only valid form. By comparison, by opposition, by distinction are not standard discourse markers.
7   …showed _____ the same improvements…
Correct: B — broadly
Trap: Register discrimination. Broadly the same is the formal, academic hedging expression meaning approximately or generally the same — standard in research writing. Nearly the same is neutral but slightly informal. Mostly the same focuses on proportion rather than degree. Roughly the same is informal and implies approximation in measurement — appropriate in spoken or casual register, not academic prose.
8   …may be _____ more important than density…
Correct: D — far
Trap: Degree adverb before comparative. The key is the modal may be — the authors are making a cautious but strong claim. Far more important signals a large, confident degree of difference and is the standard formal intensifier before comparatives. Rather more is British formal hedging — too understated for a conclusion. Somewhat more signals a small difference — too weak for the intended emphasis. Quite more important is ungrammatical — quite modifies adjectives directly (quite important) but cannot precede a comparative form.
Multiple Choice Cloze — Text 2 of 2
Technology and Human Productivity Questions 9 – 16
A series of studies examining the impact of digital tools on workplace productivity has produced results that are 9_______ less dramatic than the technology sector's own projections had implied. The latest report 10_______ sharply with earlier research that had championed unrestricted digital access as a straightforward productivity multiplier. Its authors noted that the outcomes bore 11_______ resemblance to what proponents had promised, and called earlier claims 12_______ question. However, the new findings were broadly 13_______ with a growing body of independent research suggesting that the relationship between technology and performance is highly context-dependent. The report stands in 14_______ contrast to the optimism of a decade ago, when digital transformation was widely seen as an unqualified good. The productivity margin attributable to digital tools was 15_______ than the researchers had anticipated, and the gap between high-performing and low-performing teams appeared to differ 16_______ more by management quality than by technology access.
9   …results that are _____ less dramatic than…
Correct: B — considerably
Trap: Degree adverb before comparative. Same pattern as Q1 — considerably is the neutral academic intensifier for comparatives. Deeply modifies emotional or values-based adjectives (deeply troubled) not comparatives. Strongly does not modify comparative adjective phrases. Largely modifies verbs or adjectives directly (largely irrelevant) but not comparative constructions.
10   The latest report _____ sharply with earlier research…
Correct: C — contrasted
Trap: Complementation + collocation. Contrast sharply with is a fixed academic collocation — the adverb sharply collocates almost exclusively with contrast in this frame. Differed takes from, not with. Conflicted with implies contradiction between claims, not a stylistic or directional comparison. Disagreed takes a human or organisational subject — a report cannot disagree.
11   …outcomes bore _____ resemblance to…
Correct: B — no
Trap: Fixed collocation + uncountable noun. Bear no resemblance to is a fixed expression. Resemblance is uncountable here — few modifies countable nouns only. Slight resemblance is possible in a different sentence but the context states the outcomes were nothing like what was promised — total absence, not partial similarity. Scarce modifies countable nouns or is used predicatively, not as a pre-modifier before an uncountable noun.
12   …called earlier claims _____ question…
Correct: D — into
Trap: Fixed collocation. Call into question is the fixed phrase. Call under question is not a standard expression. In question means currently under discussion — it cannot follow called in this active frame. Call to question is not idiomatic in standard British English.
13   …the new findings were broadly _____ with…
Correct: C — consistent
Trap: Semantic precision + complementation. Consistent with is the standard academic phrase meaning in agreement with or supported by — exactly what is needed when findings align with other research. Comparable with means similar in size or degree, not alignment of conclusions. Compatible with means not contradicted by — possible but weaker; it implies absence of conflict rather than positive agreement. Compliant with refers to rules or regulations, not research findings.
14   The report stands in _____ contrast to…
Correct: D — stark
Trap: Register and collocation. Stark contrast is the C1 collocation for a dramatic, undeniable difference — especially used in formal writing to emphasise how dramatically two things diverge. Sharp contrast is also correct and frequently used — see note below. Strong contrast is possible but less idiomatic in formal prose. Great contrast collocates in literary or descriptive contexts, not academic analysis.
15   The productivity margin was _____ than anticipated…
Correct: B — narrower
Trap: Semantic precision. A margin is conceptually measured in width — it is wide or narrow, not big or small. Narrower margin is therefore the precise, idiomatic collocation. Smaller margin is commonly used and understood, but less precise at C1 level. Lesser margin is archaic and not standard in modern academic English. Slimmer margin is used in political or business contexts but is informal.
16   …appeared to differ _____ more by management quality…
Correct: A — substantially
Trap: Degree adverb modifying a verb. Here the adverb modifies the verb differ directly — not a comparative adjective. Substantially is the precise formal collocate for differ in academic writing: differ substantially is the established phrase. Considerably more naturally modifies comparatives (considerably more) than verbs directly. Largely and greatly do not naturally collocate with differ in formal prose.
