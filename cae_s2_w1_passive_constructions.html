Session 2: Removing the Scaffolding

In our last session, we built the map of the passive system. You looked at all the forms, learned their names, and practised manipulating them when given a clear prompt.

Today, the scaffolding is gone. There are no key words, no transformation labels, and no hints telling you what structure to use.

CAE Part 2 (open cloze) tests whether you can read a sentence, understand its underlying grammar, and produce the missing word entirely from scratch. The missing words in passive constructions are exactly the small grammatical words your brain tends to filter out as background noise: was, been, being, had, be, by. Today, we challenge that habit directly.

There is one rule above all others for Part 2: ONE word per gap. No exceptions. A contraction (like can't) counts as two words. A phrasal particle cannot be joined to its verb. One single word.

Part 2 is an open cloze: a single text of approximately 150–160 words with 8 numbered gaps. Each gap requires exactly one word.

Unlike Part 1 (multiple choice), there are no options. You must supply the word from your own grammatical and contextual knowledge. The words tested are almost always grammatical (function) words: auxiliaries, prepositions, articles, pronouns, conjunctions, relative pronouns, and discourse markers. Content words (nouns, adjectives, main verbs) are almost never gapped.

When a passive structure is gapped, the missing word is typically one of: was, were, been, being, be, had, have, has, by.

The signal is always in the surrounding words. Common patterns:

  • [gap] + past participle → missing auxiliary (was, were, had, been, have, is, are, will)
  • modal + [gap] + past participle → missing "be"
  • have/has/had + [gap] + past participle → missing "been"
  • is/are/was/were + [gap] + past participle → missing "being" (continuous) or direct passive
  • passive verb + [gap] + agent → missing "by"

The strategy: when you see a past participle, look left. What auxiliary is missing?

One word only — this is non-negotiable in the exam.

  • Contractions: Writing a contraction (don't, can't) counts as two words and scores zero.
  • Phrasal verbs: Never write a phrasal verb particle in the same gap as the verb itself.
  • Overthinking content: If you find yourself wanting to write a noun or adjective, stop and re-read the sentence. Part 2 gaps are structural, not lexical.
  • Leaving blanks: Always write something. A wrong answer and a blank both score zero, but a guess might be right.
