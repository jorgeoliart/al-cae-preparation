Welcome to CAE

You passed FCE. That proves your English works out in the real world. But CAE is a different kind of challenge.


FCE tested whether you could use English to get your point across. CAE tests whether you can control it. At B2, you could often find the right answer just by "feel"—because it sounded right. At C1, that instinct stops working. The structures are more complex, the traps are subtler, and the examiner expects deliberate manipulation of the language, not just good guessing.


Our main job for these first two months is to build the system behind the language. When you see a structure, you shouldn't just guess; you need to be able to name it and know exactly what to do with it.


We're starting today with passive constructions. Not because they're easy, but because they are everywhere in the exam and form the absolute foundation of formal written English. Let's get to work.

The Passive System

Key: Active Verb / Passive Auxiliary Past Participle
Present simple passive
am/is/are + past participle
Active They publish the results every year.
Passive The results are published every year.
Past simple passive
was/were + past participle
Active They built the bridge in 1987.
Passive The bridge was built in 1987.
Present perfect passive
have/has + been + past participle
Active She has submitted the report.
Passive The report has been submitted.
Past perfect passive
had + been + past participle
Active Someone had destroyed the evidence.
Passive The evidence had been destroyed.
Future passive (will)
will + be + past participle
Active They will announce the results tomorrow.
Passive The results will be announced tomorrow.
Present continuous passive
am/is/are + being + past participle
Active They are analysing the data.
Passive The data is being analysed.
Past continuous passive
was/were + being + past participle
Active They were renovating the building.
Passive The building was being renovated.
Modal passive
modal + be + past participle
Active We must address the issue.
Passive The issue must be addressed.
Modal perfect passive
modal + have + been + past participle
Active They should have caught the error.
Passive The error should have been caught.
⚠️ CAE Trap: Modal perfect passives have three auxiliaries in a row (modal + have + been + participle). Students frequently drop 'been' in Part 4 transformations. Don't be that student.

These two structures are interchangeable in meaning. CAE Part 4 will frequently ask you to convert between them. Common reporting verbs used: believe, say, report, consider, know, expect, claim, think, allege.

Structure A (It-clause)
It + passive reporting verb + that-clause
Active People believe that the policy has failed.
Passive It is believed that the policy has failed.
Structure B (Infinitive)
Subject + passive reporting verb + to-infinitive
Passive The policy is believed to have failed.
Causative have
have + object + past participle
Active A mechanic repaired her car.
Causative She had her car repaired.
Causative get
get + object + past participle
Active Someone stole his phone.
Causative He got his phone stolen.
Passive of phrasal verbs
Object becomes subject; particle stays attached
Active They called off the meeting.
Passive The meeting was called off.
⚠️ CAE Trap: When transforming a phrasal verb into the passive, the most common error is forgetting to include the particle (e.g., writing "was called" instead of "was called off").
