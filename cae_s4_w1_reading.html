Session 4: Cohesion Surgery

Yesterday was about extracting meaning. Today is about understanding how a text is built. CAE Part 6 is not about comprehension in the traditional sense—it is about following the threads of language that connect one paragraph to the next.

Those threads are exactly what we have been building this week: passive constructions. When a passive omits the agent, the missing paragraph often supplies it. When a pronoun refers back to a noun that was only introduced as the object of a passive clause, the chain only holds if the correct paragraph is in place.

Before you start, scan the base text. Mark every passive construction and every pronoun. Two minutes. The grammar is the map.

WHAT PART 6 TESTS

Identification of precise linguistic connections between the base text and missing paragraphs. Topic matching is not enough.

COHESION SIGNALS

  • Reference words: it, they, this, these, such approach, those findings.
  • Passive Continuity: If a sentence begins with "This", the thing it refers to might be the object of a passive clause in the previous paragraph.
  • Agent Chain: When an agent is omitted (e.g., "The tools were repurposed..."), the next paragraph often clarifies who did it or how.

This enthusiasm has not been uniformly shared. The question of who owns the organisms that are engineered — and who profits from their outputs — has been raised repeatedly in academic and policy circles, without having been definitively answered.

G

The distinction between synthetic biology and genetic modification is not always clearly drawn, and the two terms are sometimes used interchangeably in public discourse. Genetic modification typically refers to the insertion or deletion of specific genes, whereas synthetic biology involves the design of entire genetic circuits or organisms from standardised components. The conflation of the two has contributed to public confusion and, in some cases, to regulatory inconsistency.

Building Life from Scratch

Synthetic biology is one of the most consequential scientific developments of the past quarter-century, yet it remains poorly understood outside specialist circles. At its simplest, it involves the design and construction of new biological parts, devices, and systems — or the redesign of existing ones — for purposes that range from the production of medicines to the creation of organisms capable of breaking down environmental pollutants. The field did not emerge fully formed.
